What we built

The Morelity Platform was a subscription product that turned consumer and property data into targeted marketing audiences for lenders — the specific households a lender should be talking to, refreshed monthly. Alongside the audience delivery, customers got self-serve visualization and comparison tooling so their own marketing and analytics teams could interrogate the data rather than wait on a report.

The commercial motion was deliberately narrow and hands-on: a six-month pilot contract signed electronically, a setup wizard, a live onboarding session, then the customer's first audience requests. Pilot partners committed to monthly feedback and campaign-outcome tracking, which is how the product learned what was actually working in the field.

The market: reverse mortgage

We focused on the reverse-mortgage segment of mortgage lending — a market where identifying the right borrower is genuinely hard, the data is fragmented, and a generic marketing list performs poorly. That focus is what made a small company credible to serious counterparties.

  • Reverse Market Insight — the industry's data and analytics authority in reverse mortgage, a pilot and referral channel partner.
  • Open Mortgage — a national lender, in pilot contracting.
  • Inbound demand came through the product site itself, alongside direct enterprise selling.

The data and machine learning work

Morelity ran a dedicated data science team, with a Data Scientist to Senior Data Scientist track and researchers recruited out of academic labs. The unit of work was the audience: predictive modeling over consumer and property data to identify likely reverse-mortgage borrowers, delivered at production cadence rather than as one-off analyses.

  • Domain research fed the models. We worked from academic research on reverse-mortgage take-up, credit constraints and tax-and-insurance default — including work by Prof. Stephanie Moulton at Ohio State — rather than treating the problem as a generic propensity exercise.
  • Industry data standards were in scope. In 2018 we took on the GSE Uniform Mortgage Data Program specifications — the Uniform Appraisal Dataset and Uniform Loan Application Dataset — as the basis for interoperable lender data work.
  • Distributed ledger. I authored and negotiated a three-way partnership proposal to develop shared intellectual property around a ledger system for mortgage data, intended to start inside one partner's platform and grow into something integrable across others.

Outcome

Morelity was acquired by K4 in 2018. I stayed through the transition and handed off the product, the customer relationships and the partnership work.

Concept to revenue to acquisition, in a category where most analytics products never get past the pilot conversation.

What I take from it

Morelity taught me the thing that still shapes how I build: in regulated, data-heavy industries, the model is the easy part. The hard parts are earning access to the data, being specific enough about a segment that domain experts take you seriously, and designing a delivery cadence a customer can actually act on every month. A prediction nobody can operationalize is a demo, not a product.
